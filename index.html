Advanced Research

Computer Science and Mathematics

RESEARCH QUESTION: How can the use of complex gearing systems—such as continuous variable transmission, centrifugal continuously variable transmission, and derailleur gearing—financially optimize differential swerve drive for autonomous robotic applications?

The study

I am running a simulation study of a differential swerve drive module that replaces one of its two motors with a continuously variable transmission. A standard differential swerve pod needs two motors because both of the things it has to control — wheel speed and steering rate — are linear combinations of the two input shaft speeds. My claim is that one motor plus a variable ratio can span that same output space, which would cut the actuator count of a holonomic drive base roughly in half and take most of the cost with it. The work itself is a MATLAB modeling and simulation study: I build kinematic and power models of four chassis variants — baseline differential swerve, servo-controlled CVT, single-speed-band centrifugal CVT, and multi-speed-band centrifugal CVT — run all four through the same path-following benchmark, and compare them on achievable velocity set, energy per meter, and bill-of-materials cost. The deliverable is the comparison and the model, not a robot. If the simulation holds up and there is time left over, the plan extends into building one physical pod and checking the model against it.
